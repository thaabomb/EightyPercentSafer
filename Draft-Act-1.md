If you lived through the 90's you probably remember seeing a handful of plane crashes in the news every year. But around the year 2010, plane crashes stopped appearing in the news. Where did all the plane crashes go?

Beginning in the 1970's, the fatal accident rate in commercial aviation began to decline. This was thanks to the hard work of many dedicated and talented individuals. But by the 1990's the fatal accident rate had begun to plateau. Experts were predicting that the number of flights was going to double in the coming years. Double the flights times a stuck accident rate equals twice as many plane crashes in the news and that's bad. Something had to be done. So in 1996 President Bill Clinton issued an Executive Order challenging the aviation industry to reduce the fatal accident rate by 80% in 10 years.

In order to meet this goal, two enterprising experts joined forces. They were FAA administrator David Hinson and United Airlines CEO Edmond Soliday. They created CAST, the Commercial Aviation Safety Team, a collaboratibe voluntary committee comprised of representatives from every corner of the aviation industry. And they did it! Together, they reduced the fatal accident rate in aviation by 83% in 10 years. But how?
