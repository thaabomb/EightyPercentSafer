Yes. I found the provision you’re remembering, and your recollection of the late 1990s is correct.

The federal statute

The key provision is 49 U.S.C. § 40123 — “Protection of voluntarily submitted information.”

It was enacted by § 402(a) of the Federal Aviation Reauthorization Act of 1996, Pub. L. 104-264, signed October 9, 1996. The statute was specifically created to protect voluntarily submitted aviation safety and security information from disclosure. The official U.S. Code identifies the provision as having been added by Pub. L. 104-264, §402(a), 110 Stat. 3255. 

The operative language says, in substance, that the FAA Administrator shall not disclose voluntarily provided safety or security information when the Administrator determines that disclosure would discourage future voluntary reporting and that withholding it is consistent with the FAA’s safety and security responsibilities. 

Official U.S. Code — 49 U.S.C. § 40123⁠￼

And this is specifically tied to FOIA

The original 1996 statute didn’t simply say “FOIA” in the text. It said “notwithstanding any other provision of law.” In 2024, Congress amended §40123 to make the FOIA connection even more explicit by adding the words:

“including section 552(b)(3)(B) of title 5”

That is the FOIA provision concerning statutes that specifically exempt information from disclosure. 

So the current statute is even clearer than the original 1996 language.

The implementing FAA regulation

The statute directed the FAA to issue regulations. The FAA implemented it through 14 CFR Part 193 — Protection of Voluntarily Submitted Information. 

Part 193 specifically defines “voluntary” information and expressly includes disclosure under the Freedom of Information Act within its definition of “disclose.” 

The FAA’s rule establishes criteria for protecting information, including that:

1. It was provided voluntarily.
2. It is safety- or security-related.
3. Disclosure would inhibit future voluntary reporting.
4. Receiving the information assists the FAA in fulfilling its safety/security responsibilities.
5. Withholding it is consistent with those responsibilities. 

The original legislation

The actual 1996 law is:

Federal Aviation Reauthorization Act of 1996
Public Law 104-264
October 9, 1996
110 Stat. 3213

Section 402(a) added 49 U.S.C. § 40123. The Congressional Record/House report also reproduces the proposed §40123 language. 

Public Law 104-264 — Federal Aviation Reauthorization Act of 1996 (Congress.gov PDF)⁠￼

So I’m confident this is the law you were thinking of: 49 U.S.C. § 40123, enacted in 1996, implemented by 14 CFR Part 193.

One important caveat: it is not a blanket exemption for anything someone voluntarily gives the FAA. The statutory and regulatory criteria have to be satisfied, and the FAA has to designate/protect the information under Part 193. That distinction can be quite important in a FOIA dispute.
